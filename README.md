# Pension Tax Relief Calculator UK — What the Government Adds

> Basic-rate relief is added automatically. Higher-rate relief is **not** — you have to claim it, and millions of pounds go unclaimed every year.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

Relief arrives differently depending on which scheme your employer runs.

**Relief at source.** Your provider claims 20% and adds it to the pot. A higher or additional rate taxpayer is owed another 20% or 25% and must claim it through Self Assessment or by writing to HMRC.

**Net pay.** Taken from gross before tax, so full relief is immediate and nothing is claimed. But it gives no relief at all to anyone earning under £12,570, because there was no tax to relieve.

**Salary sacrifice.** Full relief plus National Insurance.

| Your band | Cost of £100 in the pot |
|---|---:|
| Basic 20% | £80 |
| Higher 40% | £60 |
| £100,000–£125,140, 60% effective | £40 |
| Additional 45% | £55 |

## What this does not cover

Relief is capped at the annual allowance of £60,000 or 100% of earnings, whichever is lower, counting employer contributions. It tapers to £10,000 for high earners.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
